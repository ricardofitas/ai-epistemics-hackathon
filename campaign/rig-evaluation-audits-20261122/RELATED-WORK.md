# Related work and candidate decisions

- **Prompt and scorer sensitivity.** Alzahrani et al. vary multiple-choice order, answer symbols, scoring methods and demonstrations on MMLU and other datasets. Some interventions move models by up to eight ranks. Molfese et al. compare automated extraction with human judgments and show that extractors and response-format instructions change the score. The WinoGrande experiment here fixes responses for the scoring comparison and changes the key, including when the input no longer identifies a correct answer. The starting manuscript already performs that intervention, so this campaign tests replication on a new slice and two smaller checkpoints. [Alzahrani et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01781v2) · [Molfese et al., Findings of ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.950/)
- **Leaderboard sensitivity to data.** Huang et al. estimate how few human or model preferences must be removed to change a Bradley–Terry ranking. Their focus is adversarial deletion, including in Chatbot Arena and MT-Bench. The HELM audit instead tests a fixed target population derived from a published evaluation design, with exact-match outcomes and an independent official key. HELM is already explicit about evaluating models in shared scenarios, and its MMLU release describes changes between standardized HELM MMLU and narrower HELM Lite subject coverage. An externally specified mix plus paired uncertainty is a stronger case study than an arbitrary subset search. Both rank instability and the importance of task selection are established. [Huang et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11847) · [Liang et al., HELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110) · [Stanford CRFM's MMLU release](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2024/05/01/helm-mmlu.html)
- **Reproducibility of measured comparisons.** Sarkar's self-audit of model-inferred prompt structure measures drift across identical calls, instability under resampling, and sensitivity to merge precedence. A frozen replay with exact score recovery addresses archival reproducibility for a single measurement, while held-out items assess a different source of variation. [Sarkar, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30074v1)
- **Paired controls and partial identification.** WinoGrande was constructed with adversarial filtering, and prior Winograd work exploits paired consistency to probe model artifacts. For a given fixed A/B prediction pair, a missing answer can be enumerated jointly across the two keys. A paired model margin on recorded scores is guaranteed to withstand fewer arbitrary row deletions than the absolute net count of discordant wins, or fewer key changes than half that count, assuming unit question weights. These bounds are elementary. An important empirical question is whether a useful fraction of deployment-relevant comparisons can be certified with incomplete archives, or whether realistic missingness leaves most undecidable. That would affect which leaderboard entries researchers can act on or prioritize for recovery. The starting evidence already gives missingness bounds case by case, and Huang et al. are the closest published analogue for deletion robustness. [Sakaguchi et al., WinoGrande](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10641) · [Elazar et al., EMNLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.819/) · [Huang et al., ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11847)
- **Benchmark weights and transport.** Ailem et al. test several non-uniform weighting schemes on four benchmarks and show that prompt weighting can change rankings. That is the closest direct precedent for the MMLU scope result. Testing an externally defined, five-subject target with preserved model outputs provides an independently reproducible case study of known sensitivity. [Ailem et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16966)
