# Independent audit of HELM MMLU v1.0.0

The five-subject target changed 22 of 231 model orderings relative to the published 57-subject ranks. Eight of the 23 first-pass reversals pointed in the same direction on the held-out questions. None had an unadjusted paired 95% interval excluding zero on that half, and none passed the frozen adjusted threshold. The main track claim would require evidence across task mixes and benchmark archives beyond this case study.

Pair order is lexical by model ID. The two reversal columns reflect only this bookkeeping choice. All 22 published configurations were recoverable across the five subjects. Each of the 22 full published exact-match aggregates equalled the average of its 57 published subject scores. All 110 model-by-subject published target scores reproduced from the archived per-item metrics, covering 11,308 archived requests. We retrieved 222/222 archive objects.

The original five-subject test set has 514 rows. The same normalized question and ordered options occur twice in US foreign policy at official zero-based indices 32 and 43. Both official labels are D. The archived instances `id48` and `id59` could each be either source row using the frozen content join. We excluded both in all 22 configurations, leaving 11,264 unambiguously bound responses and 512 questions. There were 112 unparseable completions among the matched observations. Each received zero under both keys and the score agreed with HELM. All matched reference labels agreed with the official MMLU key. Matching by itself, strict extraction by itself, and official-key replay by itself each changed zero five-subject orderings. The 22 descriptive reversals came from the narrower subject mix.

The five subjects were set by the published HELM Lite design. The bootstrap captures paired item variation within those fixed subjects and models. The published comparator is the 57-subject table, which includes the target questions. The assessment partition checks the direction in new target items relative to that fixed table. It does not independently estimate the baseline or generalize to unseen subjects. The margins on the held-out panel change too little relative to their item uncertainty to support selecting a different model from this result alone. If a deployment decision depends on a narrower task mix, evaluate both candidates on matched questions for that mix and report the margin and uncertainty directly.

The largest design limitation is that a five-subject restriction changes the target estimand by construction. Subject specialization is sufficient to produce descriptive reversals on a clean leaderboard. Ailem et al. are the closest published precedent and establish that benchmark weighting changes model ranks. A future test that could change the assessment is to freeze a task mix and compare matched pairs under independently observed reporting omissions or key discrepancies, with enough paired data to separate these effects from sampling variability.
