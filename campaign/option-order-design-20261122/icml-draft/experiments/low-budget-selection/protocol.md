# Prospective benchmark transfer of an eight-call design selector

The targets are the paired full-order accuracy gap and the MSE of its estimate on a fixed question set. We compare the certified eight-call samplers for position plus precedence (P) and full directed adjacency (W). Positive signed risk difference means that W has lower MSE. Development used the published HellaSwag, MMLU and LogiQA 3B profiles. None of the four transfer panels was used to choose the rule. The older ARC-Challenge and OpenBookQA validation panels used a different model pair.

## Admission and split

Use publisher validation data for ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA, Cosmos QA and RACE. Keep exactly four distinct nonempty options. Exclude duplicate normalized question texts, explicit references to options above or below, and the specified references to option letters. Include the passage with the question on RACE and Cosmos QA. Order eligible questions by a fixed hash and use one per normalized passage or standalone stem. The total character screen is 1,450 and all 24 prompts must produce distinct token sequences of fewer than 512 tokens in each model. Keep 256 questions per primary cohort. Of 406 RACE passage candidates after the character screen, 135 qualified for both tokenizers. Its 128-question panel is the shorter transfer test, retained under the same prompt and inference protocol. Group names and eligibility use question metadata alone.

Use Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and SmolLM3-3B with the same pinned Q4_K_M weights, numeric prompt, single-token decoder and physical RTX 4060 Ti as the matched study. A separate SHA-256 salt assigns one quarter of each roster to calibration. The remaining three quarters are held out. Both roles are disjoint by question and by passage. The benchmark rosters, roles, random pilot orders and study code are fixed before inference. Every complete reference covers all 24 orders on both models. Commit the sparse choices before scoring the held-out responses. If interrupted, resume identical verified fixtures. No changes to size, cutoff, methods or benchmark inclusion after seeing outcomes.

## Sparse calibration

The primary rule uses the residual pilot in pilot.py. Half of its probability is spread uniformly over the 15 contrasts between Williams blocks. The other half is spread uniformly over six orthogonal signed residual directions. Each direction uses eight distinct orders. On the three common supports the score combines both squared contrasts with their inclusion weights. Its expectation for each profile is exactly the eight-call P risk minus W risk. It cancels constant, position and precedence effects on every draw. Choose W if the mean sparse score on calibration questions is strictly positive. Ties go to P. Both models see only the same eight assigned orders per calibration question for this decision. No fitted coefficients or learned margin are used.

A prespecified secondary pilot samples P itself and uses the 28 observed order pairs with inverse joint-inclusion weights. This score has the same expectation. Its calibration gap estimates have the same risk as fixed P. The primary residual pilot showed lower signed-score dispersion on the three development panels. Its sampling law can increase the MSE of calibration-item gap estimates, which is charged in the full-panel comparison. Each policy uses its own eight calls and does not combine observations from the other. The hypothetical full-profile calibration score on the same calibration questions serves only as a diagnostic upper-information comparator and costs three times as many pilot calls.

## Outcomes and uncertainty

For each prespecified choice, compute the exact conditional MSE of P and W on held-out questions using the 24-order reference. Report the chosen design, its agreement with the lower held-out MSE, its regret against the better fixed rule, and its relative improvement over always using P. Report ties explicitly. A table of panel-specific 95% paired percentile bootstrap intervals for the held-out W gain describes sensitivity to question composition. Also calculate simultaneous Bonferroni intervals across all four panels by using 0.625% and 99.375% quantiles in each, with 50,000 common item resamples per panel. Each question is its own passage group. These intervals do not quantify generalization to a distribution of benchmarks.

Report end-to-end risk for the finite panel with calibration included in the accuracy-gap estimate and all calls charged. Each candidate policy uses eight shared orders per question. Integrate calibration-item gap risks exactly. Average held-out deployment risk over the pilot choices using 20,000 independently randomized pilots on the fixed calibration questions. Report Monte Carlo standard errors for selection probabilities, fixed P and W at equal cost, and expected regret on the held-out questions. The independent held-out gap error has zero conditional mean given calibration, so the variance contributions add even when the selected design depends on the calibration responses. The single registered decision and rerandomization frequencies answer different questions. The registered pilot choice and primary held-out results are reported whatever their direction.
