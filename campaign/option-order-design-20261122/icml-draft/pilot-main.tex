\subsection{Choosing a sampler on a new benchmark}
\label{sec:pilot}
Can a small calibration set identify which eight-call sampler will
estimate the model gap more precisely? We constructed an unbiased
estimate of the risk \emph{difference} from eight shared orders on
a single labeled question. A signed contrast from two Williams
blocks measures the variation that survives adjacency balance. Six
further signed contrasts measure the variation that adjacency removes.
Sampling from these contrasts cancels position and precedence effects
on every draw (Appendix~\ref{app:sparse-pilot}). We average the
resulting scores over calibration questions and choose adjacency
when the mean is positive.

We registered this rule on ARC-Challenge \citep{clark2018},
OpenBookQA \citep{mihaylov2018}, Cosmos QA \citep{huang2019cosmos}
and RACE \citep{lai2017}. The same Qwen and SmolLM 3B checkpoints
answered all 24 orders on 896 questions. The primary rule saw eight
orders on 224 calibration questions. Its four choices were recorded
before scoring the other 672 questions. Both calibration and test
answers count toward the deployment budget.
