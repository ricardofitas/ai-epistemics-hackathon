\subsection{Reordering demonstrations in classification}
The same permutation theory applies when the ordered elements are labeled demonstrations. Earlier work found that demonstration order affects few-shot accuracy \citep{ludemos}. We froze two tests of how precisely eight permutations estimate a paired accuracy gap. We kept class descriptions fixed and varied the order of four training examples. Each task used eight disjoint, class-balanced quartets and eight distinct validation queries per quartet. Both 3B models answered all 24 orders per query (6,144 calls). The primary comparator used eight rotations and reversals. We resampled whole quartets 50,000 times for descriptive, task-specific 95\% intervals.
 On SST-2, precedence gave 21.66\% higher MSE than rotations (signed gain interval $[-53.64,2.48]$\%). On AG News, precedence gave 31.82\% higher MSE than rotations (signed gain interval $[-62.06,-11.47]$\%). Uniform sampling needed 13 orders on SST-2 and 12 on AG News to match eight precedence-balanced orders. The comparison conditions on these short texts and model checkpoints. With eight quartets per task, the intervals have limited resolution across demonstration sets.
