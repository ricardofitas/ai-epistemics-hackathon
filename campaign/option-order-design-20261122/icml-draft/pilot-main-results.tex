Precedence had lower exact MSE on all four held-out panels
(Table~\ref{tab:pilot-transfer}). Both the registered
primary pilot and a fixed choice of precedence picked the winner four
times. Only OpenBookQA's panel-specific interval excludes a tie.
All four simultaneous intervals include zero. Repeating just the pilot
order draws on the same calibration questions yields a correct choice
with probability $49.6$--$63.8\%$, computed by exact convolution.
When the calibration questions enter the gap estimate, the primary
policy has $4.50$--$7.39\%$ higher expected MSE than fixed precedence
at identical total cost. Even a pilot that always picked the better fixed design for each
held-out panel would leave a $3.48$--$4.71\%$ full-panel penalty from calibration alone.
