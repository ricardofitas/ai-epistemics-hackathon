# Independent replay of archived TabMWP predictions

A secondary audit of the released T5 and TAPEX baselines from Lu et al., ICLR 2023, complements the prospective Adaption factorial. The ten baseline files listed in sources.json are the complete contents of these two directories and use the same multiple-choice extraction algorithm. GPT-3 and PromptPG use a different evaluator. This audit fixes the scope and alternatives after inspecting the evaluator and one example's schema, before counting archive-level discrepancies.

Bind every archived problem ID and answer to the authors' complete, pinned test split. Recount the original flags, reproduce multiple-choice predictions from raw text, and check the evaluator's numerical endpoint against the independently fixed 100 TabMWP cases in the Adaption study. An independent strict-rational score is a sensitivity analysis of the archived extracted prediction. It measures a different endpoint from a rounded answer and cannot adjudicate rationale quality.

The historical evaluator chooses the first option attaining the maximum similarity. Predeclare the subset in which *all* options have a zero similarity to the raw prediction. Retain all other original scores. On this subset compare (i) original first-listed choice, (ii) last-listed choice and (iii) abstention, with abstention scored incorrect. Compute observed shifts in published baseline files and any strict ranking inversions, on identical questions and outputs. This tests an implementation-level default in a real third-party pipeline. Equal, zero similarity under its matcher is an operational definition, not a human judgment that the text could never convey intent. Reordering only the tie-break does not measure how a model would respond to reordered options. Keep distinct baselines and shared question IDs identifiable. There is no claim about the headline GPT-3/PromptPG difference, and no attribution of the MixEval contract to TabMWP.
