## Three paragraphs for manuscript integration

Evaluation reproducibility, scorer sensitivity and semantic compatibility are established concerns, including in Biderman et al., EvalEng, SANE and Judge Reliability Harness. Upstream One-Eval PR #63 also predates this audit and explicitly reports answer-extraction and scoring fixes and a change to primary-metric headline reporting. Our contribution is therefore the documented source-to-request and reference failures, the implemented interventions and their bounded measured consequences, rather than the general idea of semantic contracts or metamorphic testing. The following TabMWP audit supplies a separate consequence in published third-party baseline results; it does not establish that the audited One-Eval defects affected an independent published conclusion.

The archive audit reproduced 76,860 released correctness flags for ten UnifiedQA/TAPEX baselines on the same 7,686 TabMWP questions, including the historical option selections for all 19,920 multiple-choice outputs. Its case-sensitive similarity matcher selects the first option when every similarity is zero. Among those zero ties, recognizing a uniquely stated option after Unicode NFKC, case folding and whitespace normalization changes 557 model-by-question correctness flags on 432 distinct questions (397 gains and 160 losses), while preserving every pairwise model ordering. This alone raises pretrained TAPEX-large accuracy from 18.59% to 21.68%. If recognition is followed by withholding credit only from residual zero ties, pretrained TAPEX-base changes from 15.73% to 11.11%, below UnifiedQA-small (12.01%) and UnifiedQA-base (14.23%): two strict pairwise reversals across the ten releases. Abstention is an explicit scoring-policy sensitivity, not a human-validated correction of every unmatched reply. These are fixed-output reanalyses of baseline releases, not new source-model generations, and neither test the headline PromptPG-versus-GPT-3 comparison nor establish the same defect in MixEval.

A registered follow-up used two model families to interpret 100 stratified TAPEX-base replies, with original model identity, stored answer, source table, score and option order hidden. The archive contains 224 terminal records and 223 complete responses; each judge passed all 12 controls, and the judges agreed on the 99 complete study pairs. Of 50 sampled cases originally credited by the first-choice default, 40 had no identifiable option, two identified the correct option, seven identified another option and one remained unknown. The corresponding counts among 50 originally uncredited cases were 42, three, five and zero. These controlled model judgments support the concern that historical credit can arise without a recoverable answer choice, while also showing that zero matcher similarity does not imply absence of meaning. They are not independent human ground truth, a full-archive corrected accuracy or evidence of population-wide capability changes.

## Scientific claim decisions

| Claim | Decision and qualification |
|---|---|
| External consequence established | Published UnifiedQA/TAPEX baseline scores and their pairwise order depend on specified rescoring conventions applied to the same archived outputs. The outcome is a counterfactual scoring sensitivity of actual historical results. |
| 557 correctness changes | Retain. These are flags, not changed gold labels, source questions or newly generated responses. They involve 432 distinct source IDs and induce zero ordering reversals alone. |
| Two robust qualified reversals | Retain under exact-option recognition plus residual-zero abstention only: pretrained TAPEX base versus pretrained UnifiedQA small and base. Do not substitute the original all-zero-abstention three-reversal result. |
| Initial three reversals | Historical secondary sensitivity only. The TAPEX-large versus UnifiedQA-large inversion is four responses and disappears after explicit-option recognition. It is not the qualified main result. |
| First-to-last tie-breaking | 901 TAPEX-base flag changes, zero aggregate reversals across 45 unordered model pairs. This is a deterministic scoring counterfactual, not a new response to reordered options. |
| Gold/meaning adjudication | Explicit normalized unique matching is mechanically well defined. Residual abstention remains policy-dependent. Blinded model interpretation is evidence with correlated-error risk, not human truth. |
| Blind denominators | 100 study questions, two judges, 12 controls per judge, 224 terminals, 223 complete responses, 99 complete study pairs. Do not describe 224 independent questions or 223 pairs. |
| Blind weighted estimate | 82.57–83.28% of the 1,238-reply zero pool is a weighted estimate of model-judge no-identifiable-option judgments. Its narrow range covers the one unknown only; it excludes sampling variation and shared judge error. Omit it from the main prose unless these limitations accompany it. |
| Main paper comparison | GPT-3 and PromptPG use another evaluator and were not tested. Do not imply the paper's headline method claim was overturned. |
| One-Eval external impact | Existing C9 remains unresolved for affected independent conclusions. The TabMWP archive is a distinct pipeline and cannot fill that provenance gap. |
| General novelty | Do not claim first scorer/default sensitivity, first evaluation contract, first metamorphic gate or first evidence that evaluation software changes rankings. General motivation and overlapping methods have prior art. |
| Comparative superiority | No direct implementation comparison establishes superiority to SANE/JRH/EvalEng or absence of their coverage. JRH comparison remains abstract-only; do not turn missing inspection into a negative feature claim. |
