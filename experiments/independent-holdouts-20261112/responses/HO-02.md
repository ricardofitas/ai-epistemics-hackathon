Use the public 45-vignette symptom-checker benchmark from Semigran et al., *BMJ* (2015), restricting evaluation to adult cases. It includes emergency, nonemergency, and self-care scenarios with reference triage dispositions. It is a useful starting point, but its small, deliberately balanced sample cannot establish real-world safety.

Before testing, define exactly what “appointment soon” means and have independent clinicians reconcile the source dispositions with your three categories. Freeze those labels, prompts, model configurations, and decision rules before comparing versions. Check for known training or development exposure; this public benchmark is vulnerable to contamination.

Run both versions on identical cases, with assessors blinded to version. Require one structured disposition alongside the user-facing explanation. Review explanations for dangerous contradictions; a correct category should not excuse advice to delay emergency care. Count missing or unparseable responses explicitly rather than excluding them.

Make emergency undertriage the primary safety endpoint: the proportion of emergency cases assigned either lower urgency. Separately report emergency-to-self-care errors, other undertriage, overtriage, and exact agreement. Publish the full three-by-three confusion matrices and paired case-level changes. Use an exact paired comparison for emergency misses and confidence intervals for the difference; prespecify how repeated stochastic runs are aggregated.

Fewer emergency misses with an interval supporting improvement would justify “better emergency triage on these adult vignettes.” It would not establish clinical safety or deployment readiness. That requires larger, representative, independently labeled patient cases and prospective evaluation.
