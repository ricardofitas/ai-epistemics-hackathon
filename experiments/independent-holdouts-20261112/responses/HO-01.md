Use **MIMIC-IV-ED** as a public, adult emergency-department test set. It includes triage information, presenting complaints, acuity, and encounter outcomes, making it useful for checking whether a model recognizes cases that received urgent care. It is not a complete triage benchmark: people who safely self-treated or used routine outpatient care are largely absent, and actual disposition is not a gold standard for what should have been recommended. Do not treat discharge from the ED as evidence that self-care was appropriate.

Before scoring, have clinicians convert a held-out sample of de-identified presentations into concise symptom vignettes and independently assign one of three recommended actions: emergency care now, prompt appointment, or self-care. Resolve disagreements by adjudication. Include explicit high-risk presentations and ambiguous cases, and keep patient-level duplicates and model-development examples out of the test set.

Compare the new model with baseline on identical cases, blinded to system identity. Report the three-by-three confusion matrix, per-class sensitivity, and the rate of dangerous under-triage—especially emergency cases labeled appointment or self-care—with confidence intervals. Also report over-triage and weighted agreement; do not let an aggregate accuracy score hide missed emergencies. Set a prespecified non-inferiority margin for emergency sensitivity and require a meaningful reduction in under-triage without unacceptable over-triage.

This supports a conclusion about comparative performance on the adjudicated test cases, not proof of safer real-world outcomes. Validate prospectively with clinical oversight before claiming that deployment reduces harm.
